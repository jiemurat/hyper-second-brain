# The monthly review

This reference holds the steps of the monthly review. The rules that hold for the whole vault are in `HYPER-SECOND-BRAIN.md`, the Project List in `../hsb-projects/references/project-list.md`, and the areas in `changes.md`; this file names them and does not repeat them.

- The steps

## What the monthly review is for

The weekly review resets the vault; the monthly review steps back to the user's goals, priorities, and areas, so that the projects keep serving what matters to the user. Put the steps to the user one or two at a time.

## The goals note

The goals note holds the user's goals for the quarter and for the year, and the user's dreams: goals they call important that have no project yet. A line under "Lasting choices" in `project.md` gives its path, or says that the user wants none.

- When "Lasting choices" says neither, ask the user once whether they keep such a note. When they do, record its path. When they want one, create it in the area folder the user chooses (an area the user has not named needs their yes first), with a title and three sections in the user's language, for the quarter's goals, the year's goals, and the dreams, and record its path. When they want none, record that, and ask no more.
- Write into the note only what the user says, in their words.

## The steps

1. **Goals.** With a goals note, go through it with the user: which goals they reached and why, what surprised them and what they learned from it, and which goals to cross off, add, or change. Write the changes the user confirms into the note. Without one, ask as "The goals note" says; when the user wants none, skip this step.
2. **Projects.** Review the Project List as a full review (`../hsb-projects/references/project-list.md` → "Reviewing the list"): archive the finished and cancelled projects, add the new ones, and update those that changed; leave the ranking to step 6. When the weekly review ran just before in this session, its review of the list stands: add only the questions of the full review about dreams and hobbies.
3. **Areas.** Ask which areas the user wants to look at, and reflect on them with `changes.md` → "Reflecting on areas and resources". Add the balance question when the user's saved material piles up in Resources while an area they hold (health, relationships, finances) stays nearly empty: ask whether that area needs more of their attention. When the user declines, record under "Lasting choices" that the question about that area is not asked again.
4. **Ideas kept for later.** Go through the notes of the parts cut from projects (`../hsb-projects/references/lifecycle.md` → "Shrinking the scope"), in Projects and in Archives, and the dreams in the goals note. Ask whether one has become actionable, and offer to start a project from it (`../hsb-projects/references/lifecycle.md` → "Projects from efforts").
5. **Recurring themes.** Find the notes added or changed since the last monthly review, or in the last 30 days when none ran: by the vault's git log, or by the file dates without git. Read their titles, open at most 20 that look most telling, and tell the user in three lines or fewer which themes recurred and what they seem to say about what matters to the user. Then ask what the user learned from experience this month that is not written down yet, and offer to save it in the user's own words as a note, placed as `../hsb-place-an-item/SKILL.md` says.
6. **Priorities.** Rank the Project List with the user, as "Reviewing the list" ranks it, now that the steps above may have added or archived projects.

Done when each step has been put to the user and each answer is applied.
