# Finding: the ways to find and to answer

This reference holds the ways to find what the vault holds and the forms of the answer. The rules that hold for the whole vault are in `HYPER-SECOND-BRAIN.md`; this file names them and does not repeat them.

## The four ways to find

No single way finds everything: go through them in this order until you find what the request needs.

1. **Search.** Run the script with several terms at once: the words of the request, their other forms and synonyms, and the same words in the other languages the vault uses. Run it again with new terms when the hits are thin. Open the hits that look like they answer, and read the lines that do.
2. **Browse.** When you do not know exactly what you are looking for, or the material is images or files: list the likely project, area, or resource folder, read its `_about.md` when it has one and the names of its files, then go to the related folders.
3. **Gather.** When the material for an effort sits in several folders and the user does not want it moved, list it in the answer, each item with its title and folder. Write the list as a note with links only on the user's yes, in the folder of the effort it serves; add a shared tag to the notes only when the vault already uses tags and the user says yes.
4. **Look wider.** Search five or six related folders (similar projects, the areas involved, other resources) with `--in`, because the most useful connections cross subjects. Show each find by its title and the line that matters.

Done when you have named the title and folder of what the request needs, or have told the user plainly that the vault holds nothing on the subject after all four ways.

## Ranking and age

Order the finds by how well they answer the request, not by how new they are: the script ranks by matches, and you reorder the top hits by what they say. Take material of every age, and say how old an item is when its age matters, such as an old figure or a plan that may have changed since: take the date the note states, else the day its file last changed.

## Past work and evidence

When the user reviews a past period, updates a résumé, makes the case for a raise or a promotion, or pitches or proposes something, search the Archives folder first (`--in` with the Archives folder that `project.md` names), then the rest of the vault. Finished projects and their results live there.

## Where a project stands

Give the project's entry in the Project List (its goal, its timeframe, and its place on the list), the latest entry of its "Where I left off" note (a note whose title means that in the language of the project's notes), and the titles and dates of the notes in its folder that changed last. State these as facts, and name no last or next step that no note states.

## Connections and patterns

When the user asks for ideas or links, offer connections drawn from material across areas, formats, and ages, each with the notes it comes from. When the user asks about the material of one project, read it as a whole and report its patterns: themes that repeat, notes that contradict each other, and gaps, each with the notes that show it.
